General Electric: the historical roots

General Electric was formed in 1892 when J. P. Morgan organized the merger of Thomas Edison’s Edison General Electric Company and Elihu Thomson’s Thomson-Houston Electric Company. Thomson-Houston executive Charles A. Coffin became GE’s first president.

Edison’s manufacturing operations had moved to Schenectady, New York, in 1887. GE’s formation there in 1892 helped make Schenectady one of the central places in the history of the American electrical industry.

The lineage matters because it was not the story of one inventor or one product. Edison, Elihu Thomson, Edwin Houston, Coffin, financiers, engineers and factory workers all fed into a corporation that could move from lighting and electrical equipment into power generation, transportation, communications, appliances, research and major industrial projects.

What inspired Federal Electric

Federal Electric borrows the shape of that industrial story, not GE’s identity. The game begins with a small 1890 light-bulb manufacturer and leaves room to grow into components, radios and electronics, utility equipment, industrial machinery, government contracts, major infrastructure and eventually wartime production.

General Electric, Westinghouse and Sylvania are historical reference points for the kind of American electrical-industry growth the simulation is exploring. Federal Electric remains its own fictional company, with its own decisions, successes, scandals, products and alternate history.

One scale example is Hoover Dam. The Bureau of Reclamation records that the first two of the initial four 82,500-kilowatt generators were awarded to General Electric, while the other two went to Westinghouse and a 40,000-kilowatt unit went to Allis-Chalmers. That kind of leap from ordinary electrical manufacturing into enormous public infrastructure is the sort of long-term opportunity Federal Electric may eventually pursue.

How to Play

This guide describes the systems that are playable in the current Federal Electric build. Planned features are not presented here as if they already exist.

Quick Start

  1. Open Executive Desk and check Company Cash, Personal Cash and the current phase.
  2. Open Factory > Contracts and review available or accepted work.
  3. Buy the raw materials you need, choose the production grade and start the job.
  4. While production runs, decide how to spend the rest of the Business Day: manage contracts, trade stocks, visit contacts, attend the races or pursue other opportunities.
  5. Watch Company Cash and Personal Cash separately. Company money belongs to Federal Electric; gambling and personal spending use personal money.
  6. When the Closing Bell rings, ordinary daytime business closes and Washington Night begins.
  7. Use the Night for activities such as licensed boxing, poker, evening Star Club visits and other nighttime opportunities.
  8. After Night, review the mandatory Daily Report. The next business morning begins with a new Metropolitan Ledger edition.

The Main Idea

Federal Electric is the company you own.
Washington is the world you inhabit.

You are running Federal Electric in 1890 while Washington and the wider economy keep moving around you. The game is not only about maximizing factory output. Your time is limited, and business, markets, contacts, gambling, information and personal choices compete for that time.

You are not expected to do everything. Leaving the office may cost you attention somewhere else, but it may also create contacts, information or opportunities you could never obtain by staying behind the desk.

Time, Pause and the Daily Cycle

Federal Electric uses 4 playable business turns per calendar month. The main campaign runs for 372 turns, from January 1890 through September 1897. Each turn contains a 60-minute Business Day followed by a 60-minute Washington Night. Time-costing actions subtract directly from those clocks, and End Day Early remains available when you are finished.

The fixed header shows your global campaign position, such as TURN 2/372. After Turn 372, you may continue the same save in unlimited free play or start a new game.

World systems tied to that clock continue while you are elsewhere in the game. Factory production can continue during eligible Business Day time, races and fights can settle while you are away, and the hidden Federal Exchange moves during the Business Day.

PAUSE freezes the simulation, returns you to Executive Desk and disables gameplay controls. Only PLAY and New Game remain usable while paused.

Federal Electric does not advance while the game is closed. Autosave preserves the current state so you resume from the saved point rather than receiving offline progress.

Executive Desk

The Executive Desk is the company overview. It shows Federal Electric's cash, your personal cash, company value, reputation and the daily ledger.

Production shifts: the closing bell automatically stops an unfinished factory run. The line never restarts itself the next morning. You must return to the Factory and authorize Resume Production; forgotten production time is lost.

Contracts: ordinary orders generally allow four playable turns, while same-day and rush work may demand delivery sooner. Large supplier orders can span six, eight, twelve, or more turns across multiple months. Every contract shows its own delivery window and exact deadline.

Company Cash pays for business activity. Personal Cash pays for your personal activities, investments and wagers. They are separate resources.

A Recorded Owner Distribution moves money from the company to you as an acknowledged distribution. Divert Company Funds also moves money to you, but creates unaccounted company funds and accounting exposure that later systems may react to.

End Day Early advances the current day early. New Game is the destructive reset and requires confirmation.

Factory: Production

Production creates goods for the Warehouse; it does not automatically fulfill customer contracts. You choose a production target, grade and production policy, and finished bulbs enter warehouse inventory as they are manufactured.

Light bulbs currently have Economy, Standard and Long-Life grades. Workforce attendance, skill, morale, discipline, machinery condition, weather and production policy can affect speed and quality.

You can maintain a production queue and switch runs. Switching away from an unfinished run requires confirmation; already-manufactured bulbs remain in the Warehouse and the run can be resumed later. Utility outages and machinery trouble can stop or slow production while the Business Day clock continues.

Factory, contract and supplier activity closes during Washington Night.

Factory: Contracts

Seek New Contracts consumes Business Day time and may or may not produce suitable work. Customers specify what they want; Federal Electric can negotiate quantity, grade and unit price before an offer becomes an accepted contract.

Accepted contracts normally provide a delivery window through the end of their contract month. You may manufacture early, hold several orders worth of goods in the Warehouse, ship partial quantities where the buyer allows it, or deliberately ship nothing and accept the consequences when the deadline passes.

Customers remember how you treat them. Correct deliveries, discounts, rejections, defects, partial deliveries, lateness and other fulfillment outcomes can affect later business. The game allows you to accept more work than may be wise.

Warehouse

The Warehouse holds both raw materials and finished goods. Copper, glass and carbon filaments are available in 1890; tungsten becomes available later. Materials can be bought or sold in arbitrary quantities, while completed bulbs are tracked by grade and production lot.

Production consumes materials as goods are made. Finished goods stay in Federal Electric inventory until you deliberately allocate and ship them to a customer. Lot records allow defective batches and later customer complaints to be traced back to their production run.

Markets & News: Federal Exchange

The Federal Exchange has fictional securities that become available as their industries emerge. The published opening quotation remains visible through the Business Day while the market moves behind the scenes. At the Closing Bell, the closing quotation is revealed and becomes part of the security's history.

You buy and sell whole shares using Personal Cash. The portfolio tracks shares, average cost, market value, realized profit/loss and unrealized profit/loss. There is no arbitrary game-imposed investment ceiling beyond your available money and the systems actually implemented.

Click a company name to open its security record. The current build includes approximately 90 pre-game sessions of quotation history and appends completed playable days.

Markets & News: Metropolitan Ledger

The Metropolitan Ledger is Federal Electric's newspaper view of the world. It carries financial and industrial news, Washington and society items, sports results and simulation-driven stories.

Completed editions can be archived. Important racing and boxing results can feed the next morning's Sports section.

Journal

The Personal Journal is your running record of things you experienced, were told or chose to write down. Meetings, conversations, rumors, correspondence and selected activities can be recorded automatically or manually.

A Journal entry records what your character knows or believes. It is not a guarantee that the information is objectively true. Sources may be mistaken, incomplete, self-interested or deceptive.

Contacts

The Contact File contains people you have actually met or legitimately learned how to reach. Meeting somebody does not guarantee that they will help you, answer you or tell you the truth.

Contacts can open later business, social, gambling and underworld opportunities. Different people know different things, so discovering one contact does not automatically reveal every activity.

Info

The Info tab is historical reference material. It explains some of the real electrical-industry history that inspired Federal Electric and provides source links. Federal Electric itself is fictional.

Pastimes: Legal Sports

Capital Race Grounds is a fictional Washington-area venue created for the simulation. It operates during the Business Day. The current meeting uses persistent horses, four heats, two semifinals and a Main Event. You can place Win, Place and Show wagers on established fields, and races settle on the shared clock even if you leave the track.

Licensed Boxing opens during Washington Night. Fighters have persistent records, styles, condition, injuries and medical status. Announced bouts can be wagered before their bell and resolve on the authoritative Night clock whether or not you remain ringside.

Race and fight wagers use Personal Cash.

Pastimes: Illicit Activities

Illicit Activities currently include backroom poker, the Star Club, private prizefights, cockfighting and bookmaker-driven leads.

Not every underground activity is visible from the beginning. Invitations, introductions and information from people such as Madam Star, Eddie Doyle, boxing contacts or poker acquaintances can reveal different opportunities.

The Star Club has different daytime and evening encounter pools. Poker has three circles: the Neighborhood Game, Commerce Club and the elite Embassy Room, with access progressing through contacts and introductions.

Daily Report and the Next Morning

After Washington Night, the private Daily Report summarizes important company results, stocks, personal cash, wagers, treasury activity, contacts and notable events. The report must be completed before the next business morning begins.

The new morning starts another Business Day and delivers the next Metropolitan Ledger edition.

Saving and Resuming

The game autosaves to the active local profile. The title screen supports up to three profiles, each with its own independent Federal Electric save. Continue resumes the active profile; Load Profile lets you choose another.

Closing the game does not simulate missed hours or days. When you return, the selected profile resumes from its stored state. New Game resets only the currently active profile after confirmation.

A Typical Business Day

There is no required perfect route, but a sensible first pass is: check your books, review contracts, buy needed materials, start production, then decide whether your remaining time is better spent supervising business, trading, gathering information, meeting people or pursuing recreation.

The central decision is always the same: what deserves your limited time right now? Federal Electric keeps operating in a living world, so choosing one opportunity can mean missing another.

The Star Club

The Star Club

Madam Star's discreet establishment sits behind a respectable club front. Businessmen, bookmakers, officials and policemen sometimes cross paths here.

Madam Star:

The Star Club is available during both phases, but the crowd and cost change with the hour. Daytime visits are expensive; evening visits are deliberately more costly, reflecting cover charges, drinks, tips, private rooms, and the heavier nighttime crowd. Day and night use different encounter pools. The factory continues operating only during the Business Day.

The Star Club itself is well-connected and rarely raided. A major vice raid is roughly a twice-a-year danger, not a nightly trap.
