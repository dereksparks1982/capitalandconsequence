# Political Washington · 1878

**Purpose:** Historical and alternate-history NPC roster and Washington-first political career plan. **Planning**, not an implemented political game.

## What changed in this United States

After the real 1861–65 Civil War, the fictional country abolished old states and their borders, organized the territory into purpose-based districts, and abolished the Electoral College. In our alternate history **Samuel J. Tilden** won the 1876 nationwide popular vote and is president in 1878. In actual history **Rutherford B. Hayes** held office. The 1880 result, subsequent presidents, the exact district constitution and senate selection procedure remain **undecided**.

## How players encounter real people

- **White House**: Tilden, Hendricks, authorized advisers, private office restrictions.
- **Capitol**: legislators and government visitors only when business allows.
- **Willard Hotel**: plausible lodging, public introductions, political meetings.
- **Wormley's Hotel**: politically connected lodging, private meetings, **James Wormley** as interactable owner.
- **Embassy Room**: occasional elite visitors, **not** a forced real-senator guest roster or proof any real person gambled there.
- **Star Club**: discreet wealthy guests, with its own rules; **no card gambling**.
- **Press and social events**: conversations with journalists, donors and organizations.

Use **persistent schedules, location access, relationship history, political allegiances, employment, conversation memory, and limited NPC knowledge**. The player's first encounter is not a guaranteed endorsement. A historical person's appearance at a *fictional venue* must be represented as fiction, not an archival claim.

## Political career teaching and progression

An ordinary local contact might introduce the player to a political organizer; only after credible involvement would public office become plausible. Teach eligibility and political institutions through *actions*: meetings, constituent questions, patronage, appointments, speeches, conventions, campaigns, official decisions and consequences. Possible long arcs: district commissioner/local official, congressperson, senator, president.

**Historical facts vs altered constitution:** Real 1878 senators were chosen by state legislatures (until the 17th Amendment, 1913), but **our districts replaced states**. Do not copy that selection model unchanged without an explicit game-world decision. Washington's historical elected mayoralty was abolished in 1871, so a commissioner track is an initial historically grounded design; a fictional mayor could be added only by explicit decision. Nationwide popular vote for president is established canon. No full national map/travel is approved: keep actual gameplay in Washington.

**Conflict of interest matters:** A Federal Electric owner who becomes an official may confront competing interests in public contracts. Sam, Eli and Edmund may each eventually enter public service through their own histories, but no role is forced.
