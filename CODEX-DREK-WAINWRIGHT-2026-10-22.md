# Federal Electric Character Codex: Drek and Wainwright House

**Recorded October 22, 2026. Status: design archive, NOT implemented.** Names and backstories are working possibilities, not immutable canon. This is a rare optional arc that a player may never encounter.

## The stranger on the street

**Drek** is an apparently homeless man in Washington, perhaps older, eccentric, articulate and strangely well informed. He begs, wanders, sleeps outdoors, disappears for days and chats with passersby. He may actually be a millionaire. His proposed real name is **Cornelius Wainwright III**, his proposed home **Wainwright House**. The player's first meeting with him should be genuinely **rare**; some profiles never discover him or the mansion.

Why play at poverty? No single explanation has been selected: liberation from wealthy society and responsibilities; a social experiment observing kindness to the poor; loneliness; hiding from enemies or heirs; discreet intelligence gathering because elites and ordinary citizens overlook him; or a more deceptive persona. He is **not required to be mentally ill**. He could accidentally reveal too much: finance knowledge, a private company contract, an elite acquaintance or an unexpected remark.

A special encounter, address, clue or earned invitation can **reveal Wainwright House as a permanent numbered location pin in that profile**, on the game's one unchanged Washington map. Discovery does not automatically grant entry, free residence or inheritance. Drek might be somewhere else when the player arrives.

## The British butler

At the grand mansion an impeccably dressed, **snobbish British butler**, proposed as **Mr. Pritchard**, opens the door. If the visitor asks for 'Drek', he corrects them with chilly formality: **'I believe you mean Mr. Cornelius Wainwright the Third.'** This is illustrative dialogue, not a finalized script.

Pritchard might refuse entrance, grudgingly let the visitor wait, invite them to stay, or introduce them to Wainwright. Wainwright may or may not be home. Reception, library, guest accommodation, private study and household staff are potential distinct rooms. Access depends on ongoing circumstances and relationships, not a fixed mission chain.

## Possible true identities and endings, NOT simultaneous requirements

**Eccentric millionaire:** No conspiracy. He merely prefers living on the streets and can become an odd friend, investor or patron.

**Lonely benefactor:** He seeks an heir or trustworthy companion, perhaps because he has no family or is nearing death. He may invite the player to live at Wainwright House or eventually leave money or property. Kindness might matter, but **no action guarantees wealth**.

**Washington's quiet kingmaker:** He finances or manipulates politicians, judges, lenders, newspapers, industrialists and perhaps the presidency in this **fictional alternate-history Washington**. Rather than ruling in public, he works behind the curtain using relationships, debts, campaign support, government contracts and secrets. This is a possible storyline, not a claim about real-world figures and not guaranteed to be true in every game.

**Search for a successor:** He has observed the player's independent rise through Washington and wants someone to inherit his **network of influence**, rather than merely a house or cash. Accepting the succession may mean dealing with the enemies, favors, liabilities and secrets he accumulated.

**Dangerous inheritance or impersonation:** The estate may carry debts, competitors, claimants, investigators or enemies; his claims may be exaggerated, false, or based on disputed ownership. The alleged puppet master may be less powerful, or altogether different, than he seems.

Working name, exact age, motive, house location, financial holdings, number of heirs, power and backstory **remain open**. An illustrative thought that Wainwright might secretly own 15% of Federal Electric was **an example**, not fixed stock ownership.

## Friend, rival, enemy or nobody

Drek is NOT an automatic friend or quest giver. His memory includes **how he was treated when mistaken for a poor man**. He might reward sustained trust with a guest room, library access, confidential conversation, introductions, a business prospect or consideration as heir. He might ignore a stranger indefinitely. Cruelty, threats or exploitation can breed hostility. If he has resources, he may covertly damage a player's financing, relationships or opportunities.

If a character becomes highly influential, **he may see that character as a threat instead of a successor**. He can watch a powerful Layman mob boss control Washington's underworld while Wainwright influences the respectable elite. They might cooperate, wage a quiet power struggle, or see one replace the other. Recognition should be event-driven and remembered, not triggered by a universal good/bad meter.

**Businessman:** Might discover Wainwright is a backer, creditor, shareholder, opponent or political connection. His livelihood may be at stake.

**Patrolman:** Could investigate an irregular deal, an apparent homeless man, Wainwright's secrets, a killing or the Layman's organization. He needs real clues rather than omniscient access.

**Layman:** Begins as an ordinary citizen but could buy or take over **the racetrack at which the Businessman bets**, grow into a mob boss, rival Wainwright, ally with him or inherit some of his influence. None is compulsory. Any of these may be the **unplayed autonomous character** in another protagonist's world.

Each separate player **profile is its own forward-only Washington save**. The unselected protagonists are autonomous citizens of that profile; they may encounter one another at bars, the track, businesses and city offices, and might assist, threaten, investigate, arrest or harm one another where credible world rules allow.

## The irreversible secret: killing Drek before knowing his worth

**A player may kill Drek while believing they killed an insignificant homeless man, never learn he was rich, never see his mansion pin, and permanently lose access to an inheritance or the puppet-master storyline.** This absence of reward or explanation is essential. Some playthroughs simply end his possible arc without the player knowing what they missed.

Alternatively, a later obituary in the **Metropolitan Ledger**, legal correspondence, the butler or a police investigation might reveal his real identity and the opportunity lost. **The reveal is not guaranteed and it does not undo his death.** He might instead die naturally, disappear or be killed by an unrelated NPC. Estate decisions and repercussions persist.

Maintain separate **ground truth**, **character-specific beliefs** and **public newspaper reports**. A newspaper can report suspicion or a death without immediately disclosing secrets; the player should not get spoilers the player-character did not earn.

## Necessary future simulation, still unbuilt

- Extremely rare discovery triggers and permanent per-profile location knowledge.
- Saved identity, relationships, resentment, trust, influence, money, holdings, heir claims, roles, deaths and mansion access.
- Personal schedules and physical whereabouts; he is not waiting frozen at a mission marker.
- NPC reactions to accumulated power and prior treatment, including his response to cruel street encounters.
- Legal/financial consequences of an estate or racetrack transfer; no arbitrary free-money button.
- Investigations, witnesses, false rumors, hidden facts, newspaper exposure and irreversible one-save consequences.
- Dialogue may eventually use a local AI model, but only simulation code determines actual facts and outcomes.

**No Drek character, Wainwright House, Mr. Pritchard, inheritance, puppet-master branch, map pin or rivalry system exists in the present v0.6.32 game. This is documentation-only.**
