# ELIAS CROWE · THE INHERITED BUTCHER SHOP
## Owner design record · November 3–4, 2026 · SPOILER / DEVELOPER ONLY

**Authority:** The owner's successive conversation decisions, culminating in “bake all of this in; we can't afford to lose anything.” This file preserves *both* settled details and suggestions requiring later decisions. **It is not player-visible lore.** The player must not receive a developer-only murder confession or secret relationship simply by reading the normal career guide, public UI, NPC cards, news, police files, or books.

### Settled protagonist and shop
- Elias “Eli” Crowe retains the same established **`criminal` route ID**, existing portrait, Washington birthplace, skills, personal records, and general freedom to pursue crime. **His opening profession is now an inherited butcher-shop proprietor**, **not** an employed nightclub doorman. The Golden Peacock, Lui Delacroix and approved club geography/story content must not be deleted; older automatic doorman employment assumptions are superseded.
- His father was a working butcher and an alcoholic who died under circumstances everyone initially takes for alcohol-related death. Elias had been away living a different life when his father declined.
- Elias owns and operates the shop as his legitimate livelihood. It should be a **real working butcher's** business, not a push-button perpetual money source: meat and carcass supply, cutting, price setting, customers, stock and spoilage, supplier payments, shop cash, sales records, and consequences for time away. Start with a useful real loop; record any not-yet-built submechanics explicitly.
- A single **cousin** serves as assistant butcher and **acting manager** when Elias steps away. He learned the trade helping Elias's father during summers when he and Elias were young, stayed/returned to care for the ill father later in life, knows suppliers/customers, and is competent enough to open, run and close the shop himself. The cousin **permanently lives in the upstairs loft** above the business, just as Charles's nephew lives in a loft above his factory. The cousin's personal name, age and exact familial parentage are *not* yet established. Do not invent a canonical name.
- Cousin is **not** a reluctant moral conscience. He willingly assists Elias's criminal activities, may hatch plans of his own, and **believes he is the boss**. He openly insists the shop should have been his. Family loyalty and criminal complicity can coexist with genuine rivalry.

### Disputed estate: two separate concealed crimes
- In his final years the father had made a **will leaving the shop to the cousin**, who cared for him. The cousin knew he was the intended heir.
- Elias found and **destroyed the will** after his father's death. When no will was produced, **the estate passed to Elias under the game's inheritance circumstances**, excluding the intended beneficiaries. This is hidden conduct the *Elias player-character knows*, not publicly documented proof. Do not claim every real jurisdiction automatically grants an entire intestate estate to one son; fictional estate details can later resolve local legal plausibility.
- The owner also proposed and authorized preserving the deeper mystery: **the cousin killed the father by smothering him while he was incapacitated by drink**; the death was believed to be alcoholism. The cousin wanted the shop. The exact timing, medical proof, witnesses, and provable murder evidence remain undecided.
- The owner proposed the father and cousin had **a secret romantic relationship in adulthood**. It is hidden backstory and possible discoverable evidence, never casual NPC omniscience, routine gossip treated as fact, or explicit scenes. Nothing romantic/sexual occurred during the cousin's childhood summer visits. Whether love or manipulation motivated the adult relationship is **left unresolved**.
- Cousin **suspects Elias disposed of the will** and may later obtain evidence. He avoids involving police, lawyers, courts, or other investigators, **because scrutiny could reveal his own killing**. Elias initially does not know of the killing or the adult secret relationship. Suspicion must not be equated with proof; no automatic indictment or solved case.
- This is an asymmetrical standoff: **Elias knows the will destruction; cousin knows his own killing; each has reason not to invite outside scrutiny.** Eventually, one or both secrets may be independently discovered through witnessed events, credible records, deliberate investigation and meaningful player choices, never magically injected into another character's private records.
- Do **not** solidify the assistant's speculative reason for timing the killing (“father threatened to rewrite the will”) as a proven event. It was an optional story explanation, not owner-confirmed.

### Shop conflict and recurring play
- Cousin is able to keep the shop open **while Elias is absent**, and proceeds from genuine customers must remain accounted for. His expertise may save business; he must not be a one-note fool or always wrong.
- Sometimes he **overrides Elias's price rises**, restores a posted price, extends credit/discounts to familiar customers, places different supplier orders, or **fails to enter transactions in the books**. These should have genuine, observable economic consequences and a discoverable ledger trail; no arbitrary free-money generation or clock resets.
- **Till money** is distinct from Elias's personal cash and from Federal Electric's treasury. The owner and cousin can take money from the till; an *owner draw* differs from an *unrecorded withdrawal or misappropriation* and should be reported distinctly. The user's personal anecdote about taking from his father's till is **design inspiration, not Elias's autobiographical canon**.
- Give the cousin independent **loyalty, resentment and suspicion** dimensions with nonidentical effects: loyal criminal partner yet infuriating assistant, possible competent price correction, unwillingness to bring authorities even when angry, and gradual shifts through confrontation/reward/choices.
- Cousin's loft is a permanently lived-in, privately bounded upstairs room, not a temporary guest bed. Shop floor, cutting/preparation room, storage/larder, supplier receiving door and loft comprise the intended establishment. Avoid inventing or moving the already approved Washington map pins without owner direction.
- Criminal adventures and Golden Peacock social contacts remain possible **outside** his ordinary butcher profession. Old club missions dependent on mandatory paid doorman shifts require a carefully approved future rewrite as club contacts/side arcs, **not** erasure or an accidental declaration that he remains employed as a doorman.

### Mystery design and reveal gates
1. **Opening:** Elias inherits and controls shop; cousin argues about ownership, runs it capably, and may interfere with prices or books. Public story calls father an alcoholic casualty. Elias's destroyed will is a private guilty secret.
2. **Unease:** Repeated price reversals, inexplicable till shortages and disputed records create practical trouble. These are *not automatically clues proving murder*.
3. **Investigation:** Optional credible clues about final illness, the estate, correspondence, the father's adult relationship, accounts, witnesses, and contradictory recollections. Every discovery requires its own player-observed event and records.
4. **Suspicion/knowledge:** Cousin can become confident about the will while fearing murder inquiries; Elias can uncover the secret relationship and possibly eventually evidence of killing. A suspicion is not the same as certainty, and neither is the same as legally admissible proof.
5. **Choice:** Confront, conceal, exploit for leverage, or bring evidence to authorities, each with appropriate risk to Elias's own will destruction. An uneasy mutual-secret partnership may remain possible. The mystery **never forces** the player to abandon butcher gameplay or railroad a single ending.
