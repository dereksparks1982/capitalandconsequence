## 2. This thread's approved vision, exactly
**The city lives independently of the player.** Every interactable resident should eventually possess their own persistent life: identity; adult status where appropriate; personal history; residence; employment; workweek; off-duty errands; sleep and needs; income, expenditures and savings; aspirations; acquaintances, feelings and memories; individual personality; potential life changes; and actual destinations at each point in the shared simulation. This includes ordinary residents, Star Club women, named social figures and historical figures, and the other **three unselected playable protagonists**. Do not equate a cast list, boxer roster, poker table or randomized newspaper event with a fully simulated person.

**People have schedules, not appointments with the player.** If Charlie happens to see Eddie Doyle at the Star Club, both chose to be there independently. Visiting a usual spot does not summon that person. They may work, sleep, stay home, become ill, change plans, meet other people, take invitations, or pursue their own goals. A player who hears an announced event, an invitation, a promise of a meeting, or a person's routine can purposefully seek them out. Distinguish *learned probable plans* from *hidden actual location*. Even known plans can fail. No omniscient contact tracker.

**One authoritative clock:** normal real-time ticking at 1 second per 1 game second while unpaused, with optional explicit player activity/travel jumps. The chosen protagonist's clock progression is the only clock authority; NPCs and unchosen protagonists resolve their actions within elapsed time and **never add time**. No progression while the game is closed and no old 9x/15x/two-hour rhythm. Chronological reconciliation of large player time jumps, double-settlement protection, shifts, wages, cash, witnesses and room occupancy are mandatory.

**Actual rooms, actual people:** Enter a building via its existing map pin and full-screen room. The room shows a simple list of the people **currently in the same actual room**, not everyone employed there or permanently attached to the location. On arrival, departure, room change, or time advancement the list changes. Apply identical legitimate door, duty and invitation rules to NPCs and normal players. Developer room access must not grant normal gameplay privileges.

**Conversation UI, correction repeated three times:** Select a person from that room's list; **their own separate character card** opens over the room. Inside that person's card are a generic **male or female silhouette** until the owner approves individual pictures, their identity, an ongoing dialogue transcript, and an unrestricted free-text field. **ALL conversation happens in the card, NEVER inside the room.** The room's job is finding people. Closing the card returns to the same room. If the individual leaves, the conversation ends. The card is not a bottom-room dialogue strip or a replacement room. The early first-person pseudo-3D medieval RPG conversation displays discussed in the thread are UX inspiration, not a request to recreate any one named game.

**No conversation menus or suggestions.** The player must think of what to say. NO predefined topic buttons, four-choice menus, question lists, suggested prompts, or auto-generated dialogue options, even if private legacy functions still expose four topics in source. The card takes anything the player types in ordinary language. Hundreds of character-specific authored facts, answers and situational variations will be needed over time, but they must be selected by meaning/context, not exposed as a visible menu. A local language model may supply more flexible dialogue, **never world truth or actions by prose alone**.

**Every NPC has a distinctive temperament.** Sims-like traits: sociability, patience, politeness, honesty, discretion, ambition, caution, pride/arrogance, curiosity, generosity, loyalty, confidence, class attitudes, prejudices and evolving mood/needs. People may welcome a conversation, politely decline, ignore someone, brush them off, or be rude/snobbish based on **that person's** personality, current occupation, fatigue, relationship and circumstances. Do not flatten each demographic group into the same personality. Encounters, conflicts and kindness can alter lasting trust and reputation. These interactions cannot mint money, fake invitations or create infinite relationship points.

**Historically grounded 1878 knowledge and attitudes:** People know only what someone of their **date, location, language, education, class, personal experiences and witnessed events** could know. No television, Corvettes, smartphones or foreknowledge of later historical events. Period social conventions should shape interactions without making every resident identical. On unknown questions they should say they do not know, are unsure, or decline. They can be sincerely mistaken, biased, deceptive or discreet, but they cannot secretly read game truth, another NPC's private memories or future events. The same question about the Star Club receives distinct perspectives from owner Madam Star, acquaintance Eddie Doyle, inspector Hayes, assistant Sullivan, and others, with their source of knowledge qualified. Non-English speakers require a real shared language/interpreter, not magical translation.

**Memory and consequences:** Personally encountered facts, rumor, opinion, unverified player claims and witnessed truth need separate provenance. Repeated interactions yield bounded persistence, important memories/reflections, evolving social standings, believable choices and independently earned appointments. A character's spoken assertion is not a transaction, confirmed secret, committed crime, scheduled meeting or permission grant without separate authorized gameplay logic.
